About BRAHMOS

Why this exists

CLPS Lunar Browser brings Rishi's interactive Moon map and Suhas's visibility backend together. Choose a landing site and UTC timestamp to explore sunlight, Earth visibility, and terrain context.

Terrain profile
Local obstruction context when a horizon profile exists.

This is an independent NASA Space Apps 2026 project and not an official NASA product. Lunar imagery is credited to NASA’s Scientific Visualization Studio and the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter program. The local preview interpolates hourly geometric Sun and Earth positions generated from the project’s NAIF SPICE kernels. Athena uses the bundled LOLA terrain horizon. Other locations use a flat horizon. Visibility windows report their sampling interval. Landing safety requires separate assessment.

Scientific Specifications & Data Sources

Planetary Reference Frame
Observation vectors are computed in the IAU MOON_ME_DE440_ME421 mean Earth/polar axis selenographic reference frame with mean lunar radius R = 1737.4 km and planetocentric east-positive selenographic coordinates.
Ephemeris Toolkit
Generated with NASA/JPL NAIF SPICE using de440s.bsp, naif0012.tls, pck00011.tpc, moon_pa_de440_200625.bpc, and moon_de440_220930.tf.
Topographic Horizon Modeling
Athena’s profile evaluates 720 azimuthal bins at 0.5° intervals, using the south-polar LOLA 80 m DEM. The profile extends 40 km and assumes a 2 m observer height.
Visual Mapping
The NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit supplies LROC-derived color and LOLA displacement maps for visual context.
Preliminary Orbital Mechanics
Trip transfers use an Earth-centered, short-way Lambert solution with date-based DE440 endpoints. The arrival reference is 1,000 km above the Moon’s Earth-facing side. Ascent, capture, and landing require separate models.
