# Lander eye-level surface view

In Explorer, choose an exported patch, enter surface view, then switch between **Orbit Camera** and **Lander Eye-Level (1.8m)**. The eye position is a ray intersection with the verified, true-scale mesh plus 1.8 metres along the selected coordinate's local radial up. Missing intersections disable eye mode. Eye mode fixes position: dragging/touch and arrow keys change heading/elevation without orbiting, panning, zooming, or changing the selected point. Orbit restores the previous orbit camera and allows a new point to be picked. Orbit collision clearance remains 2 metres and does not raise the eye camera above 1.8 metres.

A surface-only slider covers the next 24 UTC hours from the Explorer observation time in 10-minute increments. It preserves the model/camera while scrubbing. Changing the Explorer observation time resets the slider window without redownloading the model. Requests are debounced and cancelled; older responses cannot overwrite the current frame. The previous valid frame stays visible while updating; errors turn direct sunlight and sky objects off and expose the optional inspection light. Inspection lighting remains explicitly illustrative and off by default.

`/api/terrain/models/{id}/lighting` now accepts the fourteen exported local scene IDs as well as existing model slugs. A local scene uses its actual anchor and inverse mesh-axis rotation. SPICE Moon-fixed Sun/Earth positions provide topocentric direction and distance at that anchor. Responses add angular radii (Sun radius 695700 km; Earth equatorial radius 6378.137 km), Earth-to-Sun direction in mesh axes, and Earth illuminated fraction `(1 + cos(phase angle))/2`. The phase angle is measured between Earth-to-Sun and Earth-to-observer vectors. The display is geometric (no light-time, eclipses, atmospheric rendering, or albedo calibration).

The Sun disk and textured, shaded Earth sphere retain their computed angular sizes and are placed beyond the terrain, with normal depth testing so the mesh can occlude them. Earth surface texture orientation is illustrative; phase and sky position come from SPICE. Sky objects below the nominal geometric horizon are hidden once their upper limb is below it. The same Sun direction drives the directional shadow map. Mesh shadows and the visible horizon cover only the exported patch, not distant terrain outside it. Model sampling/triangulation limits visible detail at eye level; no rocks, fabricated elevations or detail surfaces are added.

Validation: camera-height/heading/UTC-window unit tests; real SPICE local-axis, angular-size and Earth-phase API tests; existing model-route compatibility; browser checks on the measured G7 and Apollo 12 meshes, eye/orbit controls, Sun/Earth targeting and time-scrub persistence. Local Cesium global terrain authorization is independent of this viewer and remains a deployment configuration concern.

Earth texture attribution is retained in `frontend/public/earth/sources.json`.
